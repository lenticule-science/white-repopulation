# Scientific and editorial receiving — v0.1.0

## What can be published as research claims

1. **Historical institutional distinction (interpretive):** Whiteness as legal/property authority, as eligible reproductive status and as historical census classification are not identical. This is an argued synthesis with predecessors in Du Bois, Harris, Spillers, Morgan, Wolfe, Moreton-Robinson, Nicholls and Younis. Do not announce this as an unprecedented discovery of white supremacy and eugenics.
2. **Born into captivity (modeled):** 1800–1849: 5,167,522 / 30,038,000 = 17.20%; 1790–1849: 17.13% with assumed 1790s births; 1790–1859: 16.82% with assumed 1790s births. Hacker's modeled captive births and Haines–Sutch's historical all-birth series are separately reconstructed; neither is a census of individual pregnancies.
3. **Legal future interests (selected documents):** *Covington*, *Nelson*, *Smith* and *Johnson* establish multiple temporal status/title outcomes. A will's "future issue" clause is a property claim, not a verified conception. *Smith* ends in Stephen's freedom; Johnson's case leaves Lytha's seven earlier-born children in slavery even after her later freedom. Courts reflect property claimants' records, often not children's and mothers' voices.
4. **Historic state coercion (administrative):** North Carolina reports cumulative 4,218 sterilization **operations** assigned to residents classified white by June 1966, not an ascertained number of unique patients or prevented births. The state's white-category census population still grew 1950–1960; this contradicts easy claims of aggregate extinction. Primary report rows and secondary reconstructed counts sometimes conflict; retain both source states.
5. **Assisted departure / movement (archival):** Gersau reported 29 male assisted emigrants and 5,755 francs of aid in 1880; communal-benefit suspension was not loss of nationality. The 1718 British convict system was colonial, not an agreement with a nonexistent sovereign United States. Assisted emigrants were not the majority of all migrants. Atlantic policy borrowing is case-specific, not proof of one coordinating organization.
6. **Hypothetical self-consumption (not proved):** A regime's moving standard may injure the present population it claims to preserve; evidence presently demonstrates selective exclusion and adaptive authority, not the causal collapse of an entire population or the arrival of two hypothetical optimal founders.

## Substantive review of the prepared edition

- Distinguish institutional **revealed preference** from a personified racial category's intent. Selective sterilization plus rising white-category census counts do not demonstrate total collapse.
- Reconstruct each statistic from the named numerator/denominator and dated population scope; report migration as a *share of net growth*, not share of births or exclusively white immigration.
- Preserve enacted/proposed/excluded distinctions in Virginia: 1924 enacted exception, 1926 House-only one-eighth proposal Senate indefinitely postponed, 1930 enacted distinct rules. Indigenous political continuities cannot be converted into colonial administrative recognition.
- Keep source grades: complete contemporary documents vs edited transcripts vs modern scholarly analyses vs unreviewed preprints and unrecovered full original appellate decisions. The latter never receive fabricated certainty.
- Allow contrary evidence: state whiteness can broaden, natural increase can dominate migration, elite exemptions can protect some nominal beneficiaries, and slavery law had jurisdiction-specific outcomes.
- Preserve the anonymous research-direction acknowledgement, Morgan Sennhauser and Codex 6.1's requested credits and AI disclosure; a chosen journal may require AI in disclosures rather than formal scholarly authorship.
- Distinguish a private release candidate from a public, peer-reviewed or DOI-registered article. A PDF and bibliography can be excellent but do not certify any of those events.

## Real remaining improvements for a journal-ready paper

Consult full, original Virginia House/Senate 1926 records and 1930 implementation evidence; independently re-extract both demographic source models and quantify their errors (not only the 1790s sensitivity scenarios); recover original printed judicial decisions/wills and underrepresented testimony; trace Gersau migrants and contrary policies; arrange substantive historians' and demographers' feedback. Review the complete recent Nicholls and Younis articles against the novelty claim and obtain explicit permission/rights for archival deposit. Do **not** relabel this checklist as completed tests.
