# Source retrieval and qualification record

This note records which literature registers, historical editions, and statistical sources were used to advance the **client’s research problem** and their actual review level. It is not a generic literature survey, a claim that every cited monograph was read in full, or an independently conducted meta-analysis. Bibliographic identities and claim-specific support live in the [annotated bibliography crosswalk](../../../../bibliographies/bibliography-and-evidence-crosswalk-2026-10-17.md).

## Research question preserved

The first research question is whether an ideologically imagined future reproductive type can be privileged over the supported social reproduction of people presently assigned the privileged status, and whether racial/settler property institutions can reproduce themselves even when that imagined future never becomes actual. The client's image of at least two potential ideal reproducers is a **conditional limiting thought**, not evidence that there are two real hereditary racial founders.

The connected empirical questions concern (a) demographic self-replacement of specific settler communities as against migration, (b) the proportion of births into captivity within reconstructed U.S. population counts, (c) deliberate coercion of reproduction by enslavers, (d) the capitalization and expropriation of captive reproductive futures, and (e) the legal and political continuation of settler institutions. They are not interchangeable estimands.

## Positive and counterevidence that must survive receiving

1. **Primary demographic result:** historical birth numerators and denominators yield an estimated one in six U.S.-counted births into slavery over specified 1790–1859 windows, not the client’s rough one-quarter guess. Both numerator and denominator are retrospective estimates, the 1790s national birth column is unreported, and Indigenous population boundaries matter.
2. **Relevant deliberate coercion:** Djeliid 2024 documents the subject of coerced captive reproduction. It invalidates any blanket dismissal of actual deliberately compelled conception but provides no immediate all-birth prevalence denominator from its abstract.
3. **Conflicting fertility methods:** early census child–woman ratios decline while the secular onset of total and marital fertility inferred from reconstructed births can be much later. Haines–Hacker 2006, Hacker 2003 and Hacker–Haines–Jaremski 2021 must be treated as competing methods and scales.
4. **Institutional flexibility against indefinite purity:** Virginia's 1924 statute had an Indigenous ancestry exception; a 1926 House amendment proposed broadening it to one-eighth but was **indefinitely postponed by the Senate**, not enacted. The debate documents a potential clash between apparent racial purity and elite family status, not an implemented one-eighth legal change.
5. **Migration geography:** national 1790–1860 U.S. growth is dominated by natural increase, while Canada 2023 and Australia 2024 net national growth are heavily migration-dependent. Neither result answers white settler cohort replacement across locations.
6. **Client's original central claim:** actual institutional willingness to expend the classified population in order to produce an unattained ideal remains **conjectured**, not measured. A named authority, an actual choice among alternatives and a realized reproductive outcome are required before it can be asserted as causal finding.

## Reading grades and publication integrity

- **Primary text read:** original law, letter, printed historical demographic table, administrative program statistics, first-party national estimates. Historical translations or transcriptions need archival source inspection for a high-stakes quotation.
- **Original scholarly article read:** Nicholls 2026 and Younis 2026 accessed through publisher versions; a new quotation still requires page/location verification.
- **Publisher preview/abstract only:** Djeliid 2024's original publisher abstract and first-page PDF, specific monograph chapters (Clymer, Caron), other demographic studies before their regression tables are re-extracted.
- **Existing historical archive synthesis:** Virginia Humanities, NPS and National Archives materials can document certain dates and record structure but may not replace affected communities' first-person accounts.
- **Preprint:** Alphonso 2023 does not become peer reviewed because it has a DOI or a relevant legal claim.
- **Unassayed claim:** the limiting hypothetical about the privileged population being destroyed before ideal progenitors emerge; no aggregate national fertility fraction can independently certify it.

These bounds were not applied consistently in earlier turns, when the discussion cited extensive evidence but left a shallow or missing source carrier. The [bib crosswalk](../../../../bibliographies/bibliography-and-evidence-crosswalk-2026-10-17.md), two [originally overlooked empirical problems](2026-10-17-reproductive-expropriation-source-criticism.md) and [fertility-measure conflicts](2026-10-17-white-fertility-estimand-conflicts.md) now make the distinctions explicit.

## Further original-source collection required

A submission-grade study must still inspect the 1926 Virginia original bills and legislative vote record, cases from Djeliid and Alphonso, original surviving family testimony, primary county and estate deeds and valuations of future issue, Indigenous political testimony, and full regression series/methodology for competing white fertility estimates. For every new source, record exact bibliographic identity, quoted location, which dated claim it supports, contradictory evidence, reading grade and accessible copy/restriction. Do not manufacture a favorable result by extracting only the strongest passages or refusing to count countercases.
