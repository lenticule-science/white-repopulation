# White fertility transitions and incompatible measures

The client's original proposition requires measuring **which real populations reproduce, under which historical institutions, and under which material conditions**. It is not solved by one enslaved-birth fraction or by one contemporary national immigration share. The apparent contradiction between white fertility studies is itself a scientific problem, not a defect to erase with a preferred headline.

## Distinguish the estimands

| Question | Measured quantity | Required controls and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share of U.S.-counted births entering slavery | Modeled births into captivity / reconstructed national live births in matched years | Territory, status law, births vs imports, missing Indigenous persons, numerator uncertainty |
| Natural increase | Births minus deaths | Age structure, country/local boundary and historical census |
| Net migration contribution | Arrivals minus departures divided by net total population change | Period and migrant residence definition; may exceed 100% with natural decrease |
| Local settler continuity | Surviving descendants of a defined local population, with migration treated as a separate exposure | Age, sex, family formation, mortality, class, dated white classification, interregional movement |
| Marital fertility | Estimated births per married woman or own-children reconstruction | Marriage age/prevalence, infant mortality and cohort linkage |
| Child-woman ratio | Children counted per woman in a census | Survival of children, enumeration, marriage, household composition and fertility |
| Institutional continuity | Inheritance and property claims, land jurisdiction, offices, representation and labor arrangements | Institution-specific economic and legal continuity, not only headcount |

## The historical white-fertility disagreement

[Haines and Hacker's 2006 working paper](../../../../bibliographies/haines-hacker2006.md) reports a reduction of roughly one-third in *white child-woman ratios* by 1860, and analyzes the role of marriage, land and work. [Hacker's 2003 Demography article](../../../../bibliographies/hacker2003.md) instead uses new mortality and own-children estimates, placing the long-run decline of overall U.S. fertility around 1840 and white marital fertility decline after the Civil War. They are **not statements of exactly the same estimand**, so treating one as a simple correction to the other without recomputing both would be invalid. [Hacker, Haines and Jaremski (2021)](../../../../bibliographies/hacker-haines-jaremski2021.md) re-evaluate multiple hypotheses using complete-count censuses 1830–1880 and linked households. Its regression coefficients have not yet been independently audited in this corpus.

An observed fall in child-woman ratios can occur because of later marriage or child survival changes. A white couple's fewer children need not be caused by a purity-selection program; the sources study nuptiality, land, labor economics and other causes. A claimed direct connection from economic reproduction through slavery to fertility belongs to the more specifically modeled [Wanamaker (2014)](../../../../bibliographies/wanamaker2014.md), but that study's slaveowner household-level results cannot be extrapolated into nationwide white extinction.

## Birth-denominator reconciliation

[Hacker (2020)](../../../../bibliographies/hacker2020.md) estimates 5,571,170 births into slavery in 1790–1849 and 7,437,068 in 1790–1859. [Haines–Sutch (2006)](../../../../bibliographies/haines-sutch2006.md) annual all-birth values begin in 1800: 30,038,000 for 1800–1849 and 41,732,000 for 1800–1859 in the compiled series. The 1790s all-birth total in this project is **not observed**; it is imputed as 4.52m mean population times ten years times an assumed crude birth rate of 55/1000, producing 2.486m estimated births. Varying that assumption 45–65/1000 changes the two 1790-start shares to about 16.89–17.37% and 16.65–16.99%. Those ranges are **scenario sensitivities, not uncertainty intervals** encompassing all historical estimates.

The numerator's own uncertainty and total population boundary cannot be erased by recording arithmetic to three decimal places. The compiled 1790–1849 ratio is about 17.13%, not the originally proposed 25%, within its assumed population frame. However this does not answer how much social reproduction under enslavement produced income, property or governing authority for slaveholding society. Likewise the category “all births in the U.S.” is not a tally of all persons born on lands occupied by distinct Indigenous nations. [Gibson and Jung (2002)](../../../../bibliographies/gibson-jung2002.md) explicitly identify exclusion of Indians not taxed from early U.S. census tabulations and the changing social—not genetic—racial classifications.

## An actual comparative design

**Early U.S., 1790–1860:** national growth was mostly births exceeding deaths under Haines's retrospective growth decomposition. This is a national fact, not an assessment of a local or county white settler lineage. Trace origin, parent nativity, age-specific fertility and intra-U.S. movement at county level for a specifically identified community.

**Canada 2023 and Australia 2024:** the respective official sources give roughly 97.6% and 76.4% of *national net growth* from net international migration. These populations are not synonymous with white settler populations, and the year's locally born children may themselves have immigrant parents. Comparisons require consistent immigration definitions and at least one subsequent birth cohort.

**Settler-minority African polities:** imported European administrative/military support and appropriated African land and labor can sustain a settler order even without a locally majoritarian white population. The [Ginsburgh–Jackson 2019](../../../../bibliographies/ginsburgh-jackson2019.md) chapter abstract supplies contextual comparison but no extracted fertility sample.

**The client's first proposition:** it is still necessary to test whether institutions prioritized an endlessly deferred “ideal” over actual persons' surviving reproduction. Neither a legal sterilization count, a low fertility estimate nor an immigrant growth share independently demonstrates such a choice. Search for actual institutional deliberations and compare materially less harmful alternatives.

## Material social reproduction: competing causal mechanisms for the client's first proposition (2026-10-17)

The client's original **“grind down the population”** proposition encompasses the *material conditions that permit children, households and communities to continue*, not merely court-enforced sterilization or an imaginary “pure” genetic program. Three independently sourced frameworks have importantly different causal and observational requirements.

### M1 — Accumulation consumes its own conditions of reproduction (Fraser 2016)

Nancy Fraser, [*Contradictions of Capital and Care*](../../../../bibliographies/fraser2016.md), original *New Left Review* 100 (2016), “Free-riding on the life-world,” “Housewifization” and the section on financialized care chains, develops a **structural contradiction**: a capitalist accumulation system depends upon social care and provisioning but devalues and can exhaust those capacities. She describes long nineteenth-century manufacturing hours imposed on women and children and contemporary financialized erosion of public support and family-wage capacity, including labor imported from poorer societies to fill care deficits. This describes a *possible general mechanism* by which the means of accumulation weaken their reproductive substrate. It is **not** an empirical identification of the white racial population as its special victim or an estimate of births lost.

**Testable version for a settler locality:** independently measure change in available wages, household provisioning, female/male work-time burden, child survival, age-standardized marriage/fertility and migration; compare a specifically observed policy or ownership shock with unexposed localities. Test whether favored property/labor objectives were retained **despite known alternatives that would have protected reproduction**. A mere inverse correlation between wages and births does not prove a purposive racial ideal.

### M2 — Ideals of family continuity can discipline families into bearing reproduction's costs (Cooper 2017)

Melinda Cooper's [*Family Values: Between Neoliberalism and the New Social Conservatism*](../../../../bibliographies/cooper2017.md), original Zone Books publisher synopsis and chapter map, reconstructs an alliance between free-market policy and social-conservative insistence on kinship responsibility. This supplies a plausible institutional explanation for the **concurrent public exaltation of “family” and reduction of collective provision**: extending family obligations may sustain market freedom while imposing costs upon real households. The visible publisher material does *not* license specific case claims or effect-size estimates without reading the relevant chapters and policy files.

**Testable version:** trace a named welfare or debt policy's drafting, its family-values justifications, the obligations reassigned from state or employer to households, who received material benefits, and observed changes in family capacity. Discriminate support for prestigious family *status* from actual support for births and caregiving. This could represent **institutional reproductive expendability** even when no continuously tightening genetic-purity criterion exists.

### M3 — Ordinary fertility changes may not be explained by the proposed depletion mechanism (Kearney–Levine–Pardue 2022)

The original [*Journal of Economic Perspectives* article](../../../../bibliographies/kearney-levine-pardue2022.md) documents the broad U.S. fertility decline between 2007 and 2020, including among college-educated white women, but concludes that researchers could not identify a period-specific economic, policy or social change that accounts for much of the ongoing decline beyond the Great Recession's early contribution. It proposes possible cohort-level changes in aspirations and parenting norms. This is **material counterevidence** against reading every white birth decline as a direct consequence of cost extraction, purity programs, or nationalist reproductive governance. The article's replication package and full empirical tables remain to be independently executed.

**Testable version:** distinguish age-specific *timing* shifts from completed-cohort births, parity progression, immigration/cohort composition, work conditions, housing and care costs, and persons' own expressed fertility preferences. If low fertility persists among cohorts with greater economic security, a single material-deprivation explanation fails. Any genuinely racialized regime model must explain *why* those outcomes differ from other populations under comparable institutions.

### Inferential separation, observable alternatives and publication decision

A strict test has at least four candidate explanations for a decline in an actual white-classified cohort: (a) conscious selective exclusion, (b) reproductive-care depletion through accumulation or state retrenchment, (c) household labor substitution such as in slaveholding economic models, and (d) changed aspirations, marriage/cohort pattern and demographic timing not shown to depend on the first three. A fifth is **category change**: inclusion/exclusion of people in the census “white” label without corresponding change in births. Those processes may interact, but none of their source literatures demonstrates the client's stronger *two-clock, never-achieved-ideal* limit as a population-wide historical outcome.

**Journal-oriented assay:** preregister a concrete unit (e.g. county x birth cohort x property/law regime); date real legal/policy events; record population and fertility by class, race as historically classified, nativity, gender and age; identify published alternatives actually debated; and estimate whether policymakers/owners chose property/status outcomes over better-supported reproduction. Keep *the author's claim of intent*, *actual policy*, *household decision*, *observed demographic effect* and *inferred structural result* as five separate evidence cells. Reject a causal interpretation when matched comparison or instrument cannot support it. This is an empirical study design, not a reported experiment.

## Receiving conditions for a publishable result

Reconstruct original series and methods, preserve disagreements, use individual/county cohort denominators, identify actual legal/ideological interventions, and report effect sizes and negative outcomes. A further source-only paper on these methodological conflicts could be publishable if it contributes independently reviewed reconciliations rather than repackaged abstractions.
